Why financial numerals exist

Everyday Chinese numerals (一二三) are easy to alter — 一 becomes 二 or 十 with one stroke. For contracts, cheques, receipts and invoices, China uses 大写 “capital” numerals: 零 壹 贰 叁 肆 伍 陆 柒 捌 玖, with 拾 (10), 佰 (100), 仟 (1,000), 万 (10,000) and 亿 (100 million). RMB amounts end with 元 (yuan), 角 (0.1) and 分 (0.01), and 整 (“exactly”) when there are no cents.

Counting in 万 (10,000s)

Chinese groups digits in fours: 12,880 is 一万二千八百八十 (“1 wàn 2,880”). 100 million is 一亿. That's why 120,000,000 reads 一亿二千万.

Phone-number style

When reading digit strings aloud, 1 is often said yāo (幺) to avoid confusion with 7 (qī). So 077088 is read líng qī qī líng bā bā.

FAQ

How do you write 077088 in Chinese?

Digit by digit: 零七七零八八 (líng qī qī líng bā bā). As a quantity: 七万七千零八十八.

What is 大写 used for?

Anti-fraud numerals on cheques, invoices, receipts and contracts in mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan.

Is 两 (liǎng) the same as 二 (èr)?

Both mean two; 两 is used before measure words (两个人, two people) and 二 in counting and numbers.
