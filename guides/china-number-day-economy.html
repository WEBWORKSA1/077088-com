The Number-Day Economy: how dates like 11.11 became mega-sales

China turned numerology into a retail calendar. Here's how 3.8, 5.20, 6.18, 8.8, 11.11 and 12.12 work — and how to plan a year around them.

By the 077088 Editorial Team·7 min read·Updated September 2026

Why numbers make good sale days

Mandarin is full of homophones, and dates written as numbers are easy to remember and share. A date that “says” something — 520 (I love you), 11.11 (four singles), 8.8 (dad / prosper) — gives a brand a ready-made story. Platforms learned to turn those stories into deadlines, and deadlines into sales.

The scale

Alibaba reported Singles' Day GMV rising from roughly US$0.01 billion in 2009 to US$84.5 billion in 2021. After that, Alibaba and JD.com stopped publishing headline totals. 618, which began as JD.com's anniversary sale, is generally ranked second.

What this means for sellers outside China

  • Diaspora and regional markets — Singapore, Malaysia, Hong Kong, Taiwan, and Chinese communities in North America, Europe and Australia — respond to the same dates.
  • Cross-border platforms let foreign brands sell into mainland China during these windows.
  • Cultural fluency (lucky prices, taboo-free gifts, the right greeting) is a low-cost edge.

A simple annual plan

  1. Pick two anchor moments (for example Qixi and 11.11 for jewellery; CNY and Mid-Autumn for food).
  2. Add one or two awareness moments (520, 8.8).
  3. Mark caution periods (Qingming, Ghost Month) for sensitive launches.

FAQ

What is the biggest shopping day in China?

11.11, Singles' Day.

Which number-days suit romance brands?

520 (May 20), Qixi (7th day of the 7th lunar month) and Feb 14.
