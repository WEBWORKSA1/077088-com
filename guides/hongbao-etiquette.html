Red Envelope (Hongbao) Etiquette: amounts & rules

Who gets one, how much to give, which numbers to avoid, and the manners around giving and receiving — including digital red envelopes.

By the 077088 Editorial Team·6 min read·Updated September 2026

What a hongbao is

A 红包 (hóngbāo; Cantonese lai see, 利是) is a red envelope of money given at Chinese New Year, weddings, birthdays, new babies, openings and graduations. Red stands for luck and warding off evil. What counts is the gesture — and the number.

Typical amounts (mainland China)

Recipient (CNY)Common range
Your own children¥100+ (often much more)
Parents / grandparents¥500–2,000+
Younger relatives, students¥50–200
Friends' / colleagues' children¥20–50+
Employees¥100–1,000

Ranges reflect common guidance (e.g. China Highlights) and vary widely by city, income and family. Hong Kong and Singapore amounts are traditionally smaller. Personalise with the Hongbao Calculator.

Number rules

  • Avoid 4 — 4, 40, 400 sound like death.
  • Prefer even amounts for happy occasions (“good things come in pairs”).
  • 6, 8 and 9 add meaning; 88, 168, 888 and 1,688 are classics; 520 or 1314 for romance.
  • Funerals: white envelope (白包), odd amounts.

Manners

  • New, crisp notes; no coins.
  • Give and receive with both hands.
  • Don't open it in front of the giver.
  • At CNY, married adults give to unmarried younger people and children.
  • Digital red envelopes on WeChat and Alipay are normal, especially in family group chats.

FAQ

Is it rude to give too little?

Amounts are judged against relationship and local norms; a lucky, 4-free amount matters more than size for casual acquaintances.

Can I give a gift card instead?

In many diaspora contexts, yes — present it in a red envelope.
