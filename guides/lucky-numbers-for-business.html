Lucky Numbers for Business: phones, addresses, plates & domains

Why Chinese-speaking buyers pay premiums for certain numbers — and how to choose phone numbers, addresses, plates and numeric domains that work for you.

By the 077088 Editorial Team·6 min read·Updated September 2026

Numbers as brand assets

In Chinese-speaking markets a number is part of identity: a store's phone ending in 8888, an office on the 8th floor, a plate full of 8s, a 6-digit .com domain. Auctions of lucky licence plates and phone numbers regularly draw premiums, and some buildings leave out 4th, 14th and 24th floors.

What makes a number desirable

  • Repetition: 888, 8888, 666, 999.
  • Meaningful sequences: 168 (一路发), 1688, 518, 1314.
  • Endings: the last digits matter most — 8, 88, 68, 98.
  • No 4s, no 250, no 748.
  • Memorability: patterns like AABB, ABAB, ABCABC.

Numeric domains

Chinese investors and companies value short numeric domains because digits are easy to type, remember and say across dialects. Collectors often rank numbers with no 0 and no 4 highest. Many major Chinese web brands have used number-based domains.

A checklist for choosing numbers

  1. Score candidates with the Number Checker.
  2. Say it aloud in Mandarin (and Cantonese if relevant).
  3. Check that it doesn't form a negative slang code.
  4. Test that it's easy to remember after one hearing.

FAQ

Which floor numbers do Chinese buyers avoid?

4, 14, 24 and sometimes 13; 8th floors and units ending in 8 are popular.

Are 0s bad in a domain or phone number?

Not bad, but less prized than 8, 6 or 9; collectors pay most for numbers with no 0 and no 4.
