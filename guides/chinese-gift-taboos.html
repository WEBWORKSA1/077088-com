Chinese Gift Taboos: 12 gifts to avoid

The puns behind the classic gift taboos — clocks, umbrellas, pears, green hats and more — and what to give instead.

By the 077088 Editorial Team·5 min read·Updated September 2026

The list

  1. Clocks — 送钟 (give a clock) sounds like 送终 (see someone to their death).
  2. Umbrellas — 伞 (sǎn) ≈ 散 (sàn, to part).
  3. Pears to share — 分梨 ≈ 分离, separation.
  4. Shoes — 鞋 (xié) ≈ 邪 (evil) in some readings; can suggest walking away.
  5. Knives and scissors — cutting ties.
  6. Green hats — “wearing a green hat” means a man's partner is unfaithful.
  7. White or yellow chrysanthemums — funeral flowers.
  8. Sets of four — 4 ≈ death.
  9. Handkerchiefs — tears and farewells.
  10. Mirrors — fragile and linked to bad luck in some traditions.
  11. White or black wrapping — mourning colours. Use red or gold.
  12. Red envelopes with 4s — 40, 400, 444.

Safe, well-liked gifts

  • Premium tea; fruit baskets (apples ≈ 平安 peace; tangerines ≈ 吉 luck).
  • Wine or baijiu in pairs (酒 jiǔ ≈ 久, lasting).
  • Gold jewellery for weddings, Qixi and new babies.
  • Mooncakes at Mid-Autumn; zongzi at Dragon Boat.
  • Quality items from your home country.

Etiquette

Recipients may politely decline once or twice before accepting. Offer with both hands. Gifts are often opened later, not in front of the giver. Try the interactive Gift Taboo Checker.

FAQ

Is a watch the same as a clock?

No — a wristwatch is generally acceptable, though some people still avoid it for elders.

Can I give flowers?

Yes — avoid white/yellow chrysanthemums; red roses for romance, orchids or peonies otherwise.
